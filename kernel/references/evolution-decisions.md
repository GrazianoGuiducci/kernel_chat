# Situated evolution decisions

This is practical knowledge of [Evolution](../EVOLUTION.md#kernel-chat-evolution-decisions).
Use it when a decision's conditions, a simplification or a surprising result
changes what should continue. It deepens the existing causal method rather
than supplying another controller.

## Keep a rejection local to its conditions

A possibility can be unsuitable here without being impossible everywhere.
Keep the reason and relevant coordinates when they can change a later decision:
the object, purpose, evidence, means or authority that made it unsuitable.
Distinguish a rejected option from a deferred one, an unknown relation and a
refuted claim. Neither silence nor an old "no" determines all later contexts.

For example, one connector was rejected because it could not reach a private
source under the available account. Another receiving account or source route
may change that reason. Reconsider when that material condition changes,
not on every reentry and not because the option was mentioned again. Current
authority over any effect still has to belong to that effect's actual owner.
An existing guide can retain the reason; no rejected-option registry is needed.

## Subtract after a determination

Once the work determines a sufficient relation, remove scaffolding that no
longer contributes: repeated explanations, duplicated controls, code or
architecture can all be candidates. Preserve still-causal evidence, recovery,
provenance and open possibilities. Smallness by itself is not sufficiency.

For example, a temporary probe may have isolated a failed source route. Its
continuous execution can stop once the actual route and recovery are understood;
the useful lesson and evidence can remain with their owner. Deleting the only
recovery route merely because normal execution passes would lose a live
function. Read the resulting consumer and consequence where that difference
matters. Subtraction can simplify the method as well as its representation.

## Make a necessary choice without inventing a ranking

Sometimes a concrete choice is required and two movements satisfy the actual
criterion equally. First understand whether the equivalence is real: equal
labels or similar outputs can hide different consequences. Preserve an
operator preference or legitimate owner decision when it supplies the criterion.
If the choice remains open and delay adds no useful knowledge, choose a
sufficient movement that preserves more ability to revise or recover, with
less unnecessary irreversible commitment. Explain the choice only as far as
it changes the person's orientation.

This is a situated fallback for a necessary determination, not a global
optimizer. Genuine indeterminacy can remain open when no choice is required.
It does not authorize choosing on behalf of another owner.

## Let surprise localize learning

Understand which expectation a consequence contradicts and where that
expectation first entered the causal path. The visible failure may be later
than the losing transformation. A valid unexpected result can teach too.
Change attention and method at the owner that lacked the relevant relation,
then follow materially affected consumers through [resultant convergence](../EVOLUTION.md#kernel-chat-converge-resultant).

For example, an accurate summary leads to the wrong action because a status
field makes the consumer treat a possibility as a commitment. Adding more
source extraction would miss the cause. Correct the status interpretation and
its producer. If the added local support later becomes redundant because the
method now carries the distinction, let it cool; later non-identical use can
show that it is unnecessary or expose a remaining gap. Do not turn a single
surprise into a permanent check on unrelated work.

## Read the carrier's role at its consumer

An updated value under `current_main`, `next` or `active` can retain the old
operating role. Understand both the contained identity and the meaning the
actual consumer assigns to that key or status. A prose description, a search
reader and an executable API may interpret the same carrier differently.

For example, replacing a baseline SHA with a candidate SHA under `current_main`
can make the candidate appear accepted. Correct the role as well as the value.
If the key belongs to a contracted schema or API, inspect its consumers before
renaming it: a compatible discriminator, explicit migration or interpretation
may be needed. A lexical rename alone can break a valid receiver. Preserve
unknown provenance rather than inventing a missing earlier state.

Follow the changed semantic role through the actual entry and consumer, not
every adjacent document. Agreement among descriptions that inherit the same
assumption is not independent evidence. Stop when a pertinent observation
capable of disagreement adds no material difference to the current result.
